CAPELLI SPORT CUP — SOCCER

Official Rules

WHAT THIS GAME IS ABOUT

  • Two robots play 1-vs-1 soccer.
  • The goal is to push a foam ball into the opponent's goal.
  • Two halves of 2 minutes each, with a 30-second break.
  • Robots run remotely during play.
  • All robots have a speed limit (max 40 RPM) for safety.

1. The Field

1.1 Size and Layout

  • Square field: 125 × 125 cm.
  • Flat, smooth surface with painted markings.
  • Two goals (fixed on the map with double tape) — one on each side, 20 cm width of the goal.
  • Two start circles.

1.2 The Ball

  • Soft foam ball, 6 cm in diameter.
  • Only the official tournament ball is used.

2. The Robot

2.1 Allowed Robot

  • mBot2

2.2 Robot Size and Weight

  • Footprint: must fit inside 22 × 22 cm (when the robot arms is fully extended)
  • Weight: 1.5 kg maximum.
  • Power: battery only.
  • Control: Remotely.

2.3 Allowed Attachments

  • Up to 2 side kickers (using a servo motor)— one on each side.
  • Side kickers may push or deflect the ball, but they may NOT close around it, hold it, lift it, or trap it.

2.4 Forbidden Attachments

These are NOT allowed at any time:

  • Sharp edges or metal points.
  • Rotating blades, drills, or saws.
  • Magnets, sticky surfaces, or anything that can hold the ball.

3. Match Format

3.1 Time

  • Two halves of 2 minutes each.
  • Halftime break: 30 seconds.

3.2 Halftime

  • Teams switch sides at halftime.
  • Each robot returns to its NEW start circle (now on the other side of the field).
  • The ball is reset to the center.
  • If a goal is scored in the last 10 seconds of a half, the half ends right after that goal.

3.3 Start Position

  • Each robot starts FULLY inside its own start circle.
  • The robot must face the center of the field where the ball is placed.
  • The ball is placed at the center spot by the referee.
  • Each student must stay in the area behind his goal during all the matches. Left and right sides are only for judges.

3.4 Who Kicks Off

  • First kickoff: a coin toss decides. The winner of the toss chooses to either kick off OR pick a side.
  • Second-half kickoff: the team that did NOT kick off in the first half kicks off now.
  • After a goal: the team that just got scored on takes the next kickoff. The other team should not touch the ball.

3.5 After a Goal

  • Both robots return to their start circles (Each team picks up his robot and places it back in the starting position).
  • The ball is reset to the center.
  • Then play continues.

4. How to Score

  • Goal: the ball completely crosses the opponent's goal line. +1 point.
  • Bounce-in goal: if the ball bounces off a wall or off the opponent's robot and goes into the goal, it COUNTS.
  • Own goal: if the ball crosses your OWN goal line for any reason, the opponent gets +1 point.
  • No goal (push-in): if a robot pushes its opponent into the goal with the ball, this is NOT a goal. Reset to center, and the opponent gets a free kick.

All goal calls are made by the referee.

5. Movement and Contact

5.1 Allowed

  • Light pushing (gentle, not high-speed).

5.2 Not Allowed

  • High-speed ramming. The robot's max speed is 40 RPM (see §7).
  • Lifting, flipping, or tipping over the opponent.
  • Pinning the opponent against a wall for more than 5 seconds.
  • Damaging or trying to damage the opponent.

5.3 Stalling (Blocking) Rules

  • Pinning: if a robot holds/blocks the opponent robot against a wall with no way out to move, the referee counts "1… 2… 3… 4… 5" out loud. If still pinned at 5: free kick to the pinned team's opponent.
  • Stalling on the ball: If a robot remains stationary for 10 seconds while touching or blocking the ball, a free kick will be awarded to the opposing team. The referee will verbally count down to warn the teams.
  • Goal-mouth blocking: if a robot stays still in or directly in front of its own goal for 10 seconds: free kick to the other team. The referee will verbally count down to warn the teams.

6. Fouls and Warnings

The referee has the right to ask the two teams to release the ball and step back if the match is freezed for more than 5 seconds.

The two teams must obey the instructions by moving back to free the ball.

7. How Free kicks Work in case of tie match.

7.1 Free Kick

  • The ball is placed 50 cm in front of the goal that will be shot at. (marked on the map the day of the competition)
  • The defending robot must stay in his starting position in the white circle keeping the goal free.
  • Each team has 3 free kicks (alternative kicks).
  • The robot must kick the ball even in the absence of a mechanical arm.

7.2 Reset

  • Each team is allowed 1 reset per half (2 resets per match maximum).
  • A reset is allowed when a robot is stuck, flipped, or has lost connection.
  • During a reset, the match clock pauses and both robots return to their start white circle.

7.3 Out of Bounds / Stuck Ball

  • If the ball leaves the field, OR gets stuck in the corner where neither robot can reach it:
  • The referee stops play. Both robots return to their start circles. The ball is reset to center. The match clock pauses during the reset.

8. Speed Regulation

8.1 Speed Cap

  • All robots must set motor speed at 40 RPM in their code. This is mandatory.
  • If the speed goes over 40 RPM, the robot cannot enter the match until it is fixed.

The judge will start a countdown (3 2 1 go) students will start their robots forward to cross from side to side in 7 seconds. Both robots have to move in parallel and reach the other side at the same time.

  • Any "boost mode" or hidden speed change discovered during play results in a disqualification.

9. Pre-Match Inspection

Every match begins with a quick inspection. Both teams must complete every item on this list before the match starts. The referee signs off in front of both teams.

  • Robot footprint: must fit in the 22 × 22 cm box (with arms extended).
  • Robot weight: 1.5 kg max on the scale.
  • Battery level: at least 50% charge.
  • Firmware: up-to-date.
  • No sharp or aggressive components.
  • Speed test: passed.

10. Winning the Match

10.1 End of Match

  • The team with the higher score after both halves wins.

10.2 If the Score is Tied

  • Penalty shootout: each team takes 3 penalty kicks. The team with more goals wins.
  • Sudden death: if still tied after 3 shots, teams keep taking penalty kicks one at a time. As soon as one team scores AND the other does not in the same round, that team wins.

11. Tournament Structure

  • Every team plays at least 1 round (2 halves).
  • The top teams from each group move to the second round.
  • The exact format is announced before the tournament, based on how many teams register.

12. If Something Goes Wrong

  • Robot fails (no response, dead battery, code stuck): the team can use a reset (if available).
  • No reset available: the team has 30 seconds to fix the robot. If the robot cannot be fixed, the match continues with the working robot only.
  • Both robots fail at the same time: the match clock pauses. Both teams have 60 seconds to recover. If neither robot recovers, the head referee decides the match based on the score so far.
  • Battery swaps mid-match: NOT allowed. Charge fully before each match.

13. Fair Play

  • Teams must play in the spirit of friendly competition.
  • Coaches and spectators must stay outside the field area during play.
  • Disrespectful behavior toward referees, opponents, or other teams may lead to a yellow card on the team.
  • Both teams shake hands before and after every match.
